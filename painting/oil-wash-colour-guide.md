# Panel-line / pin-wash colour guide (Winsor & Newton Winton oils)

Reference for choosing wash colours per camouflage scheme, 1/48 scale. Covers
Winton oil suggestions, the closest Tamiya Panel Line Accent Color, and the
closest Mr. Color (GSI Creos, the standard Japanese lacquer line) match for
the base paint colour itself. Only GSI Creos Mr. Color is cited — not Mr.
Paint (MRP), a different, newer Slovak brand whose similar name is a common
source of confusion in the hobby; where I could only confirm an MRP number
and not a GSI one, the cell says so rather than substituting the other brand.

**Cross-reference and example photos**: [theworldwars.net's aircraft colour
and camouflage resources](https://www.theworldwars.net/resources/) are cited
throughout below (linked per section) — they carry their own Gunze
Aqueous/Mr. Color match tables plus real aircraft photos per scheme, and were
used to check/correct several Mr. Color numbers below (their thumbnails are
hotlinked from `theworldwars.net`; credit and full-size versions belong to
that site). No equivalent page exists there for the Luftwaffe (WWII) or for
Japan, so those two sections rely on direct GSI Creos bottle listings only.

**Which colour standard is cited, and why**: FS 595 (the US Federal Standard)
wasn't issued until 1956, so it's only used here for schemes that actually
post-date it — USAAF SEA/Vietnam, and modern USN/USAF tactical schemes. For
US colours that predate 1956 (WWII USAAF/USN), the standard actually in force
at the time is cited instead: Army Air Corps Bulletin 41 (1940) and the
Army-Navy Aeronautical (ANA) Bulletin 157 (1943) numbers. FS 595 has no
jurisdiction over non-US paint at all, so it's dropped entirely for German,
British, Japanese and Soviet colours — those use their own actual national
standards instead: RLM numbers (already built into the German colour names
below) for the Luftwaffe, and British Standard 381C for the RAF. No
equivalent standard reference exists for wartime Japanese or Soviet colours,
so those sections give only the paint's own name and its Mr. Color match.

**Numbering caveat**: GSI Creos renumbers/discontinues Mr. Color shades over
the decades, and some colours have two live numbers at once (an older
open-stock line number and a newer "aircraft colour set" reissue with the
same shade) — both are given where I found both, oldest first. Where I
couldn't confirm any current bottle number with confidence, the cell says so
rather than guessing — check Scalemates or the current GSI Creos catalogue
before buying.

## General wash logic

**What "thinned" means below, vs. a "+" mix**: these are two separate steps,
and every wash gets both, just written differently depending on whether a
mix is involved:

- **"X + Y"** (e.g. "Raw Umber + Payne's Grey") means mix those two pigments
  together on the palette first — roughly 2 parts to 1 favouring whichever is
  listed first, adjusted by eye — to get an in-between hue/temperature
  neither pigment gives alone. This is genuine colour mixing, not dilution.
- **"thinned"** / **"thinned heavily"** means dilute with solvent (white
  spirit or odourless mineral spirits) only — no other paint colour is added.
  It's a consistency step (inky enough to flow into a panel line and be
  wiped back), not a colour change. "Heavily" just means a thinner mix,
  for a more restrained/subtle line on a light base colour.
- Every entry gets thinned with solvent regardless — including the "X + Y"
  mixes, which get combined first, then thinned to wash consistency. Where
  a cell doesn't spell out "thinned," assume a moderate ink-like dilution
  (thin enough to flow and self-level in the line, not paste).

- **Dark base colours** (RLM70, olive drab, dark green, navy blue): a warm
  dark brown (Raw Umber / Burnt Umber) reads as grime/shadow without going flat
  black; add a touch of Ivory Black only where you want hard definition (deep
  wheel wells, gun troughs).
- **Mid-tone base colours** (greys, tans, earth colours): Raw Umber alone, or
  Raw Umber + a little Payne's Grey for a cooler, more "oily" stain.
  Straight black usually reads too stark on a mid-tone at 1/48.
  A note on your own naming-accuracy standard: 'straight black' below always
  means Winton Ivory Black, warm/brown-toned per its actual pigment (bone
  char), not a cool black — reach for Lamp Black instead when you specifically
  want the cooler, bluer version.
- **Light/pale base colours** (Sky, white, NMF, pale undersides): dilute a
  warm brown (Raw Umber or Burnt Umber) heavily — anything darker gets
  overpowering fast on light backgrounds in this scale.
- **Blues** (RLM65/76, USN Sea Blue, Soviet light blue): Payne's Grey (itself
  a blue-black) sits naturally in the same colour family and avoids the
  "muddy brown streak on blue" look a warm umber can give.
- Thin Winton oils with white spirit / odourless mineral spirits to an inky
  consistency; drag along the line with a fine brush, let sit a few minutes,
  wipe back with a spirit-dampened flat brush or cotton bud along the airflow
  direction.

## Natural Metal Finish (NMF)

Not a single paint, so no colour standard applies — bare/polished aluminium
airframe, sometimes with distinct alloy panels left deliberately unpainted
for contrast. Spans several of the schemes above rather than belonging to
one: USAAF from 1944 once the camouflage requirement was dropped for
fighters, most postwar/Korean-War-era jets before low-viz grey schemes took
over, and late-war Japanese aircraft (IJN/IJA) where paint shortages left
much of the airframe unpainted.

**Wash approach differs from a painted scheme**: metal shows dirt, oil and
exhaust staining far more readily than paint, so panel-line washes need to
be more restrained than the equivalent painted-scheme entries above — a wash
that reads as "reasonably grimy" on Olive Drab will look absurd on bare
aluminium. Two techniques do most of the work instead of a uniform wash:

- **Panel-to-panel tonal variation** (different alloys/panels weather and
  oxidize differently): pick 2–3 of the metallic base colours below rather
  than painting every panel identically, then a very light wash on top ties
  them together without erasing the variation.
- **Directional staining** (exhaust soot, oil/hydraulic leaks): dry-brush or
  drag a heavily thinned dark mix backward from the actual source (exhaust
  stacks, wing-root fuel vents, wheel wells, gun ports) rather than running
  it evenly along every panel line.

| Surface / area | Mr. Color match | Winton oil wash | Tamiya Panel Line Accent Color |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminium skin (general) | GSI Mr. Color C8 Silver (confirmed), or Mr. Metal Color MC218 Aluminum for a buffable/more reflective finish | Raw Umber, heavily thinned (streak lightly, don't pool) | Light Gray |
| Polished/bright panels (spinner, cowl ring) | GSI Mr. Color C159 Super Silver (confirmed) | Raw Umber, extremely thinned — polished areas show any wash immediately | Light Gray |
| Steel/dark-alloy panels (firewall, some control surfaces) | GSI Mr. Metal Color MC212 Iron or MC213 Stainless Steel (confirmed) | Raw Umber + Payne's Grey, thinned | Gray |
| Oxidised/weathered aluminium patches | GSI Mr. Color C61 Burnt Iron (confirmed; also doubles as a base for exhaust staining below) | Burnt Umber, thinned | Dark Gray |
| Exhaust/soot staining (aft of stacks, belly) | No confirmed dedicated GSI "jet exhaust" shade found — verify current catalogue; C61 Burnt Iron above is the closest confirmed stand-in as a base | Payne's Grey + Ivory Black, heavily thinned, dry-brushed backward from the stain source | Black (for the darkest core of the stain only) |
| Oil/hydraulic streaking (cowl, wheel wells, gun ports) | n/a (technique, not a base colour) | Burnt Umber, thinned, dragged downward/backward from the leak point | Dark Brown |
| Panel-line definition (general) | n/a | Raw Umber, very heavily thinned | Light Gray |
